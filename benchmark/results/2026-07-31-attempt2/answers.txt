## Why it only ever fires from C

Under Language Environment, **R12 is the CAA pointer, R13 the DSA, and R4–R12 must be preserved across a call.** Non-XPLINK C touches the CAA constantly — `errno`, stack-extension checks, library vectors — so a clobbered R12 kills the very next library call or stack extension.

Your assembler test cases almost certainly reload their own base register from their own save area after the call and never look at R2–R12 again. Same bug, no symptom. That single asymmetry accounts for the entire "only from C" constraint.

The standard restore is:

```
L    R13,4(,R13)          back chain to caller's save area
LM   R14,R12,12(R13)      R14, R15, R0..R12
```

`LM R14,R12,12(R13)` loads R14 from +12, R15 from +16, then R0–R12 from +20 through +68 — all from the *same* R13, in one instruction. If that save area were bad, R14 would be garbage too and you'd fail on the `BR R14` with a consistent story. You didn't. R14 survived.

- `L R14,12(R13)` executed *before* the back-chain load, then `LM R0,R12,20(R13)` after it — R14 from the correct frame, R0–R12 from a different one.

## Second: nail down the invariant, because it decides everything

"Roughly fixed, a little past 16 KB" is loose enough to hide the opposite conclusion. Get exact byte offsets from both repros:

- If build B's cut is at `len(rec1) + (build A's cut offset)` → it's **per-record**, and the classic short-block story is live: your linker describes a text record longer than the physical block it actually wrote (BLKSIZE/DCB or a short WRITE). Program Fetch's data-chained read ends when the record ends, incorrect-length is suppressed, the remaining CCWs move nothing, and the untouched storage is zeros — which execute as S0C1. Silent by design.
- If both cuts are ~16384 from the load point regardless of how text is split → the record structure is a red herring, and the short-block story is *out*. Write-side truncation cuts within a record; it can't land at the same module offset under two different splits. Same objection kills a missing chain-data flag or a per-record CCW-list overflow — all record-tied.

## Third: check BEAR before assuming there's a cut at all

If the PSW is at module+16K but you *branched* there, the whole framing is wrong.

- Branch origin sensible, target garbage → data problem at the target.
- Branch from a location whose pointer still holds a linkage-editor-relative value → relocation. ~16 KB is then where your RLD emission stopped, not where text got cut.

One look rules this in or out.

- **DASD already wrong past ~16 KB** → your linker's text emission. Program Fetch is innocent.
- **DASD right, storage wrong** → the control record misdescribes the text; Fetch moved exactly what you told it to.

Then the trichotomy at the cut — three bugs in three different places:

| At the cut | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Zeros | Never transferred: short read, or a chain that ended early |
| Stale or repeated bytes | Wrong source — buffer wrap in the linker |
| Plausible code, adcons still linkage-editor-relative | RLD emission, not text |

## Short answer

Every check you've run is a **size** check. AMBLIST's module size, the control-record CCW counts, the load point and extent — all of those are computed from CESD/CSECT lengths and record headers. None of them look at the bytes *inside* the text records. So "the module is the full size we expect" is entirely compatible with structurally perfect records full of wrong bytes.

## Why the offset points at your linker, not at fetch

The decisive clue is the one you already have: the cut is at a roughly fixed *byte offset from the load point*, and it lands mid-record — inside a single text record in one repro, inside the second text record in another.

Channel programs cut at record and block boundaries; that's what a CCW *is*. A truncation that ignores record blocking and tracks cumulative byte position is the signature of something walking the text stream linearly — i.e. a buffer. The only thing doing that is your linker's own text accumulation/emission path. The records come out full length because their lengths were computed from the CSECT table, not from what actually got written into them.

Leading hypothesis: your text buffer is ~16 KB, and past that boundary you're emitting the wrong bytes while still writing correctly-sized records around them.

**Caveat, and it's the load-bearing one:** this rests on the assumption that on-disk *content* past the cut has never actually been verified. If you have compared it and it's clean, the argument inverts — see the fallback below.

## The one test that discriminates everything

Get the bytes past the cut from both sides — the loaded image out of a dump, and the on-disk text via `AMBLIST LISTLOAD OUTPUT=MODLIST` — and compare each against expected:

| Pattern past the cut | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Zeros | Buffer allocated, never filled — short-write path in the linker |
| Earlier text repeated | Buffer wrap / output offset not advanced. Classic, and fits best |
| Unrelated garbage in storage, correct on disk | Storage never written by fetch — *then* it's an I/O question |
| Correct bytes, wrong addresses | Not text at all — RLD coverage stopped early |

Also worth nailing down: confirm the cut offset is **byte-identical** across the two repros, not merely close. Identical kills the I/O hypotheses outright. Merely close keeps them alive.

## Fallback

If the on-disk text past the cut is correct and only the in-storage image is wrong, then the problem really is in what fetch transferred, and the place to look is a control record whose CCW entries for one record don't sum to that record's actual length, or that demands more CCWs than fetch will chain for a single record. But don't start there — you'd be guessing at a mechanism when one dump comparison tells you which layer you're in.
